- **Time** : 10:30 - 12:00 (GMT+9, KST) <br>
- **Speaker** : Tony Huynh (IBS) <br>

Integer programming is a powerful mathematical optimization model that is widely used in both practice and theory. 
In particular, it can be used to model many NP-hard optimization problems. 
Thus, we do not expect general integer programs to be solvable in polynomial time (unless P=NP). <br>
However, a beautiful conjecture of Shevchenko from 1997 asserts that integer programs can be solved in polynomial time when all subdeterminants of the constraint matrix are bounded in absolute value by a constant. I will survey known results around this conjecture, including very recent joint work with Samuel Fiorini, Pascal Gollin, Simon Keil, O-joung Kwon, Sang-il Oum, Roohani Sharma, and Stefan Weltge. No knowledge of integer programming will be assumed.
